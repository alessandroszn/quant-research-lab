# Design note — Phase 8.5: a risk-free rate (€STR) and the Sharpe ratio in the report

Status: **approved 2026-11-14.** Deviations, if any, are listed at the end. Fifth phase
of Level 8 (`docs/design/level-8-plan.md`).

## The problem

The portfolio report shows the TWR, its annualised value, the maximum drawdown and the
volatility, but no Sharpe ratio. A Sharpe ratio needs a risk-free return over exactly the same
periods, and the kind has none. The library already parses the ECB euro short-term rate
(`qrlab.data.parse_estr`): an overnight rate, per year, on the ACT/360 convention, one fixing
per TARGET business day. Turning it into period returns is "left to callers", and nothing
does it yet.

**A change to the plan.** Level 8 also listed "beta on excess returns in the checks". The
beta check needs a benchmark series, which the `portfolio_report` kind does not take, so in
the kind that change would do nothing. It moves to IDEAS, next to the benchmark input.

## Design

### 1. Overnight accrual: `qrlab.core.rates.overnight_returns` (new, a financial formula)

```text
overnight_returns(rates, boundaries, *, day_count=360, calendar) -> pd.Series
```

- **Inputs.** `rates` is an annual overnight rate per business day, in decimals. `boundaries`
  are the dates that close each period. The return over `(t_{k-1}, t_k]` is

  ```text
  r_k = prod_i (1 + R_i * n_i / day_count) - 1
  ```

  over the fixing days `i` in `[t_{k-1}, t_k)`. Here `n_i` is the number of calendar days from
  `i` to the next business day, capped at `t_k`. This is the convention of the ECB's
  compounded €STR averages: simple interest over a weekend or holiday, compounding from one
  business day to the next.
- **The calendar** (`target2_calendar`) says which days need a fixing. A TARGET business day
  without one raises; nothing is filled. Periods before the first fixing (€STR starts on
  1 October 2019) raise too.
- **Look-ahead.** The rate of day `T` is published on `T+1`. A realised return over a past
  period uses only rates published by the end of it, plus one day, so there is no look-ahead
  in a historical Sharpe ratio. The docstring says so.

### 2. In the `portfolio_report` kind (optional)

- **An `estr` input:** the ECB Data Portal file of the €STR series, as `download_ecb_series`
  stores it, with its provenance from the raw-store sidecar.
- **It requires `return_frequency = "monthly"`,** checked at validation: the Sharpe ratio is
  on monthly TWR returns, annualised by √12.
- **Excess returns:** for each TWR period, the TWR return minus the €STR return over exactly
  the same dates.
- **The Sharpe ratio:** `qrlab.metrics.sharpe_ratio` on the excess returns, with the Mertens
  (2002) standard error, which allows for skewness and kurtosis. If it is undefined for these
  moments, the report says so with the reason. The number of returns is always shown.
- **The report's summary table gains two rows:**
  - the risk-free return over the whole period (€STR);
  - "Sharpe ratio (TWR in excess of €STR, annualised)", shown as `0.85 ± 0.40 (n = 24)`.

  Without `estr`, the report is unchanged, byte for byte.
- **Outputs:**
  - `risk_free.csv`, with each period's TWR, €STR and excess return;
  - the scalars `sharpe_ratio`, `sharpe_ratio_se` and `risk_free_total`.

  All are tagged `descriptive`.

### 3. The report API

`build_portfolio_report` gains an optional `risk_free: RiskFreeSummary | None = None`, with
the source name, the per-period table and the `SharpeEstimate`. It is an addition with a
default.

## Tests (hand-calculated first)

- **One weekend.** Friday 2 January 2026 to Monday 5 January, €STR at 3.6 %: one fixing
  (Friday) with `n = 3`, so 0.036 × 3 / 360 = 0.0003 exactly.
- **One business week.** Monday to the next Monday at 3.6 %: (1 + 0.0001)⁴ × (1 + 0.0003) − 1.
- **A holiday.** Easter: the Thursday fixing covers Thursday to Tuesday, with `n = 5`.
- **Errors:**
  - a TARGET business day without a fixing raises;
  - a period before October 2019 raises;
  - unsorted or duplicated boundaries raise.
- **Sharpe ratio, with €STR at 0:** TWR returns of (1, 2, 0, 3) % give a mean of 1.5 % and a
  standard deviation of 1.29099 %. The monthly Sharpe ratio is 1.161895, and the annualised
  one 4.02492. The standard error is checked against the Mertens formula computed in the test.
- **With a constant €STR:** each period's excess equals the TWR return minus the hand
  accrual over its exact days.
- **Kind:**
  - `estr` with `return_frequency = "none"` fails validation;
  - without `estr` the outputs are byte-identical to `main`;
  - studies 1 and 3 are re-run.

## Data

You have no €STR file yet. `qrlab.data.download_ecb_series("estr", …)` stores it in the raw
store with its provenance. The ECB's terms are already recorded in `docs/guides/data-sources.md`.
Downloading is a network step: I would run it only with your OK. Until then, the tests use a
synthetic file in the ECB format.

## Out of scope (IDEAS)

- A benchmark series and beta on excess returns.
- Sortino and other ratios.
- An autocorrelation-robust Sharpe standard error in the report: the studies have one.

## Risks

- **A Sharpe ratio on few months is noisy.** The ± standard error and `n` always appear next
  to it.
- **TWR versus MWR.** The Sharpe ratio is labelled as based on the TWR, never on your money
  flows.
