# Design note — Phase 4.2 `qrlab.optimization`: portfolio construction

Status: **approved 2026-11-11.** Deviations, if any, are listed at the end.

## Goal

Build portfolios from explicit inputs with standard, verifiable methods, and define the
rebalancing policies that the backtest (4.3) will run. Optimisers amplify estimation error, so
every result states its inputs (the estimator, the window and the constraints), and the
example compares methods out of sample with the 4.1 splits, never in sample only.

## Inputs

- **Expected returns** $\mu$: a `Series` per period, required only by the methods that use it.
  The caller states where $\mu$ comes from (historical, Black–Litterman or views); nothing
  estimates it implicitly.
- **Covariance:** a `CovarianceEstimate` from 3.1, which carries its estimator. Ledoit–Wolf is
  recommended for optimisation, and the example shows why.
- **Constraints**, in an explicit `Constraints` object:
  - `long_only`;
  - bounds per asset (`min`, `max`);
  - groups (assets, `min`, `max`);
  - budget $\sum w = 1$, always. Cash is an explicit asset if wanted.
  - optional turnover $\sum |w - w_0| \le \tau$ from current weights $w_0$.

  Infeasible constraints raise before solving.

## Methods

| Method | Definition | Constraints supported |
|---|---|---|
| 1/N | $w_i = 1/N$ | raises if it breaks the bounds |
| Minimum variance | $\min w^\top\Sigma w$ | all |
| Mean–variance | $\max w^\top\mu - \frac{\lambda}{2} w^\top\Sigma w$; risk aversion $\lambda$ required. Also an efficient frontier: minimum variance at a list of target returns. | all |
| Equal risk contribution | $w_i(\Sigma w)_i = \sigma_p^2/N$, solved as Spinu's (2013) convex problem $\min \tfrac12 y^\top\Sigma y - \sum_i b_i \ln y_i$, then normalised; the risk budgets $b$ are passed explicitly (equal budgets are not assumed) | long-only, fully invested |
| Maximum diversification | $\max \dfrac{w^\top\sigma}{\sqrt{w^\top\Sigma w}}$ (Choueifaty and Coignard 2008), solved as minimum variance on the correlation matrix and rescaled by $1/\sigma_i$ | long-only, fully invested |
| Black–Litterman | prior $\pi = \delta\Sigma w_{mkt}$, with market weights, $\delta$ and $\tau$ explicit; views $P, Q$ and $\Omega$, explicit or He–Litterman $\Omega = \operatorname{diag}(\tau P\Sigma P^\top)$; posterior $\mu_{BL} = [(\tau\Sigma)^{-1} + P^\top\Omega^{-1}P]^{-1}[(\tau\Sigma)^{-1}\pi + P^\top\Omega^{-1}Q]$ and $\Sigma_{BL} = \Sigma + [(\tau\Sigma)^{-1} + P^\top\Omega^{-1}P]^{-1}$. It returns $\mu_{BL}$ and $\Sigma_{BL}$, and the weights come from mean–variance. | as mean–variance |
| Hierarchical risk parity | López de Prado (2016): correlation distance $d_{ij} = \sqrt{(1-\rho_{ij})/2}$, single-linkage clustering (SciPy), quasi-diagonal ordering, then recursive bisection with inverse-variance weights | long-only, fully invested |

- **Solver.** SciPy SLSQP handles the constrained problems. The turnover constraint is made
  smooth with auxiliary variables $u_i \ge |w_i - w_{0,i}|$.
  - After solving, every constraint is checked within 1e-8, and a failure raises with the
    solver message. A result that only "looks" optimal is never returned.
  - `cvxpy` is not added. SLSQP is enough for these problems, and a new dependency would need
    a documented reason.
- **Output:** `OptimizationResult`, with:
  - the weights and the method;
  - the inputs used: estimator, $n_{obs}$, $\lambda$, $\tau$ and the constraints;
  - the objective value;
  - the volatility contributions (from 3.1);
  - the active bounds and the solver iterations.

## Rebalancing policies

A policy decides **when** to trade and **to what**. Running it over history, with weight drift,
costs and taxes, is the backtest's job (4.3), which uses these same objects.

- `CalendarPolicy(every)`: rebalance to target every `every` periods.
- `ThresholdPolicy(band, to)`: rebalance when any $|w_i - w^*_i| >$ `band`. The `to` argument
  is `"target"` (back to target) or `"band"` (only to the edge of the band, as in Masters
  2003).
- `trade(current, target, value, costs)`: the trades, the turnover and the cost of one
  rebalance. Costs are an explicit proportional rate plus a fixed fee per trade.

Taxes on rebalancing need a cost basis over time, so they are computed in the backtest (4.3),
reusing the average-cost rules of 3.3. This narrows the roadmap item "with costs and taxes" to
costs here and taxes in 4.3.

## Hand-calculated tests

Two assets with $\sigma$ = 20 % and 10 % and $\rho$ = 0.3, so $\Sigma$ = [[0.04, 0.006], [0.006, 0.01]]:

| Case | Expected |
|---|---|
| Minimum variance, budget only | $w = \Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1 / \mathbf 1^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1$ = (0.105263, 0.894737) |
| Mean–variance, $\mu$ = (8 %, 4 %), $\lambda$ = 4, budget only | $w$ = (0.368421, 0.631579); return 5.4737 %, variance 0.012211 |
| Equal risk contribution | $w \propto 1/\sigma$ = (1/3, 2/3) for two assets with any $\rho$; contributions equal within 1e-10 |
| Maximum diversification | (1/3, 2/3), the same as inverse volatility for two assets |
| Hierarchical risk parity | inverse variance = (0.2, 0.8) for two assets |
| Black–Litterman, $w_{mkt}$ = (0.6, 0.4), $\delta$ = 2.5, $\tau$ = 0.05 | $\pi$ = (6.6 %, 1.9 %). The view "asset 1 beats asset 2 by 5 %" with He–Litterman $\Omega$ moves the spread from 4.70 % to 4.85 %, exactly halfway. With no views, $\mu_{BL} = \pi$. With $\Omega \to 0$, the view holds exactly. |
| Mean–variance with $\mu_{BL} = \pi$ and $\lambda = \delta$ | returns $w_{mkt}$ (reverse optimisation) |
| Long-only minimum variance where the unconstrained solution shorts | the short asset is held at 0, a bound is active, and the result matches a hand-solved corner |
| Turnover limit 0.10 from $w_0$ | $\sum |w - w_0| \le 0.10$ and the objective is no better than without the limit |
| HRP on a block-diagonal covariance (two clusters) | weights split between clusters by inverse cluster variance |
| Threshold policy, band 5 %, target 60/40, drift to 66/34 | rebalance; `to="band"` gives 65/35 |
| Calendar policy every 12 | trades at periods 12, 24 and so on, never in between |
| Trade cost, 0.10 % proportional plus 2 € fixed, 10,000 € moving 60/40 to 50/50 | turnover 0.2, traded 2,000 €; cost 2 + 2 + 2 = 6 € (two trades) |

## Edge cases

- **Covariance:** singular (minimum variance still works with bounds; the unconstrained
  version raises); one asset; zero volatility in an asset (raises for methods that divide by
  $\sigma$).
- **Constraints:** bounds that cannot sum to 1, or groups inconsistent with bounds (raise before
  solving); a solver failure (raises with the message).
- **Inputs:** $\mu$ with labels that differ from $\Sigma$ (raises); NaN anywhere (raises).

## Risks

- **Estimation error.** Mean–variance on sample estimates concentrates in noise (Michaud
  1989). The example measures it out of sample with 4.1 splits and compares 1/N, minimum
  variance, ERC and HRP; the docs say that 1/N is a hard benchmark (DeMiguel et al. 2009).
- **In-sample flattery.** No method's in-sample Sharpe ratio is reported as evidence; only
  out-of-sample results, with their sample size.
- **Solver tolerance.** Constraints are checked after solving, and failures raise.

## New dependencies

None. `scipy.optimize` and `scipy.cluster` are already available.
