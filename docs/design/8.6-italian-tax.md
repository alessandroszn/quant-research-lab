# Design note — Phase 8.6: the Italian tax position (analytics, not tax advice)

Status: **draft, waiting for approval.** Deviations, if any, are listed at the end. Sixth phase
of Level 8 (`docs/design/level-8-plan.md`).

## Purpose and limits

This phase describes the tax position of a portfolio held by an Italian resident individual,
from the transactions qrlab already reads:

- which realised gains are *redditi di capitale* and which are *redditi diversi*;
- the tax due on them;
- the balance of capital losses that can still be used (*zainetto fiscale*);
- the reconciliation with the taxes the broker withheld.

It is **analytics, not tax advice**. Every figure is reconciled with what the broker records,
and every rule is stated with its source. Where a rule is unclear, the phase stops and asks.

## The rules, with sources (as checked on 14 November 2026)

- **The law in force.**
  - For 2026, the law is the TUIR (D.P.R. 917/1986).
  - From 1 January 2027, the new *Testo unico delle imposte sui redditi* (D.Lgs. 19 giugno
    2026, n. 117) applies. Its art. 377 sets the start date, and it carries the same rules
    under new numbers.
  - The correspondences were checked in the text published in the Gazzetta Ufficiale:

    | Rule | TUIR 1986 | D.Lgs. 117/2026 |
    |---|---|---|
    | Capital income (redditi di capitale) | art. 44 | art. 46 |
    | Other income (redditi diversi) | art. 67 | art. 76 |
    | Capital gains, and carrying losses forward "non oltre il quarto" period | art. 68 | art. 77 |

  - The engine names both numberings in its notes.
- **The reform is not in force.** L. 111/2023 (art. 5) plans one category of financial income,
  which would let losses offset fund income. As of November 2026 no implementing decree has
  done so. D.Lgs. 148/2026 (GU 11 August 2026) touches financial income only for dividends paid
  to foreign pension funds and for black-list holdings. The engine therefore keeps today's
  split, and the rules carry a date in the code, so that a later change is a visible update.
- **The split.**
  - **ETFs and investment funds (OICR):** a gain on selling units is *redditi di capitale*.
    It is taxed in full and cannot absorb losses. A loss on them is *redditi diversi*, and goes
    into the losses balance.
  - **Shares, bonds, ETCs and ETNs:** gains and losses are *redditi diversi*.
  - **Using the losses:** they offset only *redditi diversi* gains.
  - **Expiry:** losses are carried forward to the fourth tax year after the one in which they
    were realised. They expire on 31 December of that year.
- **Rates.**
  - The standard rate is 26 % (D.L. 66/2014, art. 3).
  - Italian and white-list government bonds are taxed at 12.5 %. Their gains enter the base at
    48.08 % (26 % × 48.08 % = 12.5 %), per D.L. 138/2011, art. 2, and D.M. 13 December 2011.
  - For a fund, the 12.5 % applies to the share of its assets in those bonds, a percentage the
    fund publishes every six months.
  - The 48.08 % mechanics for losses will be checked on the primary text before coding.
- **Regimes.**
  - **Amministrato** (D.Lgs. 461/1997, art. 6): the broker withholds the tax and keeps a
    losses balance for that account only.
  - **Dichiarativo:** you declare the tax yourself, and the losses go into the tax return.
  - **Brokers differ.** Some foreign brokers act as a withholding agent (*sostituto
    d'imposta*) for Italian clients, so the account is amministrato; others leave Italian
    clients in the *dichiarativo* regime. Which one applies is confirmed on the account's own
    documents.
- **Non-harmonised funds.** A fund outside the EU UCITS framework, such as an ETF domiciled in
  the US, is taxed at progressive IRPEF rates, not at 26 %. This phase flags such funds and
  computes nothing for them.

## Design: step 8.6a, the analytics on the ledger

1. **Taxable gain of each sale.**
   - The ledger's `RealisedGain` is net of everything charged on the sale, including the tax
     withheld, but the taxable gain is net of fees only. So the engine takes the fees and
     taxes of each sale transaction separately: taxable gain = gain + withheld tax.
   - The cost is the weighted average cost including purchase charges, the method of the
     amministrato regime, which the ledger already uses.
   - Foreign-currency amounts use the rates of each trade date, as the ledger already does.
2. **Classification from the instruments file:**
   - `etf` and `fund`: OICR;
   - `etc`, `stock` and `bond`: redditi diversi;
   - a fund domiciled outside the EU or EEA: flagged as non-harmonised.

   A fund needs its white-list share in `[parameters.tax.white_list_share]`, by ISIN. An
   equity fund may declare 0. A missing share raises, because no default is assumed.
3. **The losses balance (`zainetto`).**
   - Losses are kept by the year they were realised. They are used oldest first and expire on
     31 December of year + 4.
   - The balance belongs to the account: one per account in amministrato, and one common
     balance for the dichiarativo accounts.
   - The regime is declared in `[parameters.tax] regime`. Today the kind reads one broker
     export, so there is one regime. Several accounts wait for multi-export support, a gap
     already found in 8.1.
4. **The tax due.** For each sale: its category, its taxable base and the rate applied
   (26 %, or 12.5 % on its share). The amount offset with losses is stated.
5. **Reconciliation.**
   - **Amministrato:** the tax computed for each sale is compared with the tax the export
     records on that sale. A difference is shown with the sale it belongs to; it is never
     hidden or forced to zero.
   - **Dichiarativo:** nothing was withheld, so the tax is labelled "to declare (estimate)".
6. **Outputs, only with `[parameters.tax]`.**
   - A report section titled "Tax position (analytics, not tax advice)", with the sources and
     the date of the rules.
   - `tax_events.csv`, one row per sale, with category, base, offset, rate, tax due and tax
     withheld.
   - `losses_balance.csv`, with year, amount, used, expired, remaining and expiry date.
   - The scalars `tax_due`, `tax_withheld` and `losses_remaining`.
   - Without `[parameters.tax]`, the outputs are byte-identical.

### Step 8.6b, separate, after 8.6a

The loss carry-forward in `TaxModel`, for projections and backtests. It is done the Italian
way: an ETF portfolio's gains cannot use its losses, so the model needs the split, not just a
carry-forward. It gets its own short note once 8.6a is merged.

## Tests (hand-calculated first)

A sequence over five years, in one amministrato account:

1. **2021:** sell stock A at a loss of 1,000. The balance is 1,000, from 2021, expiring
   31/12/2025.
2. **2022:** sell ETF X at a gain of 2,000. It is capital income: tax 520. The balance is
   unchanged.
3. **2023:** sell ETC Y at a gain of 600. It uses 600 of the 2021 losses: tax 0, and 400 of
   the 2021 losses remain.
4. **2024:** sell ETF X at a loss of 300. This loss is *redditi diversi*: 300 more, from 2024,
   expiring 31/12/2028.
5. **2026:** sell stock B at a gain of 500. The 400 from 2021 expired at the end of 2025. The
   300 from 2024 is used, leaving a taxable 200: tax 52. The balance is 0.

In total: tax due 572, losses expired 400.

Other cases:

- **12.5 %.** A government bond with a gain of 1,000 has a base of 480.80 and a tax of 125.01.
  A bond fund with a 40 % white-list share and a gain of 1,000 pays
  1,000 × (0.6 + 0.4 × 0.4808) × 26 % = 206.00.
- **Tax added back.** A sale with 10 of fees and 520 withheld has a taxable gain equal to the
  ledger's gain plus 520.
- **Reconciliation.** A withholding of 520 against a computed 520 differs by 0. A wrong
  withholding is shown with its sale.
- **Flags and errors:**
  - a US-domiciled ETF is flagged and has no tax computed;
  - a fund without a white-list share raises;
  - a sale on a date not covered by the rules' date raises.

## Out of scope (IDEAS)

- Dividends and interest: their withheld taxes are listed, not recomputed. That includes
  foreign withholding and treaty rates.
- The stamp duty (bollo) and IVAFE.
- Foreign currency held as *redditi diversi* (the 51,645.69 EUR threshold).
- Several accounts and exports: multi-export support comes first.
- Tax-return forms (quadri RT, RM, RW).

## Risks

- **Legal rules change.** The rules carry their sources and their date. A sale after a rule's
  known validity raises instead of being taxed under assumed rules.
- **A wrong figure that looks authoritative.** Every figure is reconciled with the broker. The
  section title says "analytics, not tax advice", and differences are shown, never absorbed.
- **Real data.** The tests are synthetic. Your real tax figures stay in `data/real/`.
