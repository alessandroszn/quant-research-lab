# Design note — Phase 5.3, study 4: rebalancing for a EUR ETF investor

Status: **approved 2026-11-12.** Deviations, if any, are listed at the end.

## Question

A euro investor holds a world-equity ETF and a euro government-bond ETF in fixed target
weights. How often, and by which rule, should the portfolio be rebalanced once costs, Italian
capital-gains tax and the currency effect of unhedged foreign equity are counted?

Rebalancing is mainly risk control: it keeps the portfolio near the risk the investor chose.
Each trade costs fees and, when it sells a gain, tax. The question is the trade-off between
staying near the target and paying for it.

**Hypotheses, stated before running:**

- **H1 (bands).** A 5 % threshold band keeps the equity weight as close to its target as
  quarterly calendar rebalancing, with less trading. In terms of median paired differences:
  - the mean absolute deviation of the equity weight from target is not larger;
  - costs plus taxes paid are lower.
- **H2 (taxes).** With Italian taxation (26 % on realised gains, no offset of losses), monthly
  calendar rebalancing ends with a lower after-tax terminal wealth than annual rebalancing.
- **H3 (risk control, not return).** Buy-and-hold ends with a higher after-tax terminal wealth
  than annual rebalancing, but with more risk: a higher final equity weight, higher volatility
  and a deeper maximum drawdown.

**How each hypothesis is judged.** On the simulated paths, a hypothesis counts as supported if
its stated direction holds in at least two thirds of the paths. This is a summary rule fixed in
advance, not a significance test: the paths come from one resampled history. The median paired
difference and its 5–95 % range are always reported, together with the single historical path.

An honest null result is a valid result and is reported as such.

## Data

### Assets (EUR, monthly)

**World equity, unhedged:**

- Source: the Ken French Developed market, Mkt-RF + RF (USD, monthly), converted to EUR with
  the ECB reference rate at each month end: $r^{EUR}=(1+r^{USD})\,S_{t-1}/S_t-1$, where $S$ is
  USD per EUR.
- It stands in for an MSCI World ETF, before fees. There is no free index source with a
  licence that allows storing the raw data, so it is a proxy.

**Euro government bonds:**

- A constructed constant-maturity **7-year AAA zero-coupon bond**, rolled monthly, from the ECB
  AAA spot curve.
- **Formula:** the ECB technical notes state that the curve is continuously compounded, so the
  bond priced at month end $t$ earns
  $r=\exp\!\big(z^{7}_t\cdot 7-z_{t+1}(7-\Delta)\cdot(7-\Delta)\big)-1$, where:
  - $\Delta$ is the time between the two dates (Actual/365.25);
  - $z_{t+1}(7-\Delta)$ is interpolated linearly between the 6- and 7-year spot rates.
- It stands in for a euro government 5–10 year ETF. The AAA curve is a lower-yield, lower-risk
  universe than "all euro area", which is stated as a limitation.

**Month ends:** each series uses the last published observation of the month. Nothing is
forward-filled; a month without an observation stops the run.

**Sample:** October 2004, the first full month of the curve, to the last month in which all
series are available, about 260 months.

## Method

### Portfolio and investor

- Target weights: equity/bonds **60/40** (main) and **80/20** (robustness configuration).
- Initial investment: €100,000.
- No contributions or withdrawals, so time-weighted and money-weighted returns coincide.

### Policies (fixed now)

| Name | Rule |
|---|---|
| `buy_and_hold` | never rebalance |
| `calendar_monthly` | rebalance to target every month |
| `calendar_quarterly` | every 3 months |
| `calendar_annual` | every 12 months (the **reference**) |
| `band_5_target` | when any weight is more than 5 points from target, trade back to target |
| `band_10_target` | the same with a 10-point band |
| `band_5_edge` | 5-point band, but trade only back to the band's edge |

### Costs and taxes

**Costs**, two scenarios through the engine's `CostModel`:

- low: 0.05 % of the amount traded plus €2 per trade;
- high: 0.20 % plus €10.

**Taxes:**

- 26 % on realised gains, with average cost per asset and no offset of losses. This is the
  engine's model and matches the Italian rule that ETF gains cannot absorb losses.
- **Liquidation at the end:** after-tax terminal wealth also pays 26 % on each asset's
  unrealised gain. Every policy is therefore compared as if fully sold, so deferral is valued
  correctly.
- **Simplifications, stated in the report:**
  - the reduced 12.5 % rate on government-bond ETFs is ignored, which overstates bond taxes
    for all policies;
  - the 0.2 % annual stamp duty is ignored, since it is nearly the same for every policy.

### Metrics per policy and path

- after-tax terminal wealth and annualised after-tax return;
- annualised volatility and maximum drawdown of monthly net returns;
- mean and maximum absolute deviation of the equity weight from target, and the final equity
  weight;
- annual turnover, number of trades, costs paid, taxes paid and the deferred tax at the end.

### Historical path

- All policies are run on the actual 2004–2025 monthly returns.
- One path, so this is a descriptive case.

### Simulation

- Stationary bootstrap of the joint monthly equity and bond returns: mean block 12 months, so
  that a year of momentum or mean reversion is preserved.
- 2,000 paths of 240 months (20 years), fixed seed.
- Every policy runs on the **same** paths, compared with `calendar_annual` path by path.

**Timing.** The rules look at the drifted weights at each month end and trade at that month's
close, using only information already known at that point. The report states that this
assumes trading at the observed close.

- **`data.ecb`:** two new pinned series, the AAA 6- and 7-year spot rates, checked for their

- **`backtest.engine`:** `BacktestResult` gains `final_basis`, the net-ledger cost basis per
  asset at the end, needed for the liquidation tax. Hand-calculated tests are added; existing

- **`studies.rebalancing`** (pure functions):
  - converting equity to EUR;
  - rolled zero-coupon returns;
  - month-end sampling;
  - running each policy through the engine;
  - liquidation value;
  - path metrics;
  - bootstrap comparisons and tables.
- **Experiment kind `study_rebalancing`:**
  - inputs: the four files;
  - parameters: target weights, policies, cost scenarios, tax rate, initial value, horizon,

  - outputs: tables tagged `descriptive` (historical path and simulation), an HTML report and
    the record.

## Risks

- **Proxies.** Index and constructed-bond returns are not ETF returns: there are no fund fees
  or tracking difference. Those are equal across policies and cancel in the comparisons.
- **One history.** The bootstrap resamples 2004–2025, a period with falling then rising rates
  and strong US equity. Other regimes could rank the policies differently.
- **Specification search.** Policies, costs, taxes, block length, horizon and the
  two-thirds rule are fixed here. Two configurations are counted in the ledger.
- **Run time.** 7 policies × 2 cost scenarios × 2,000 paths is 28,000 engine runs of 240
  periods. If it is too slow, the engine's period loop will be vectorised for constant targets
  in a separate, tested step. The study will not quietly use fewer paths.
