# Design note — Phase 3.3 `qrlab.simulation`: projections and planning

Status: **approved 2026-11-11.** Deviations, if any, are listed at the end.

## Goal

Project a portfolio forward under stated assumptions and answer planning questions:

- How might savings grow?
- How likely is a goal?
- How long can withdrawals last?
- How much do the answers depend on the assumptions?

Results are always **distributions** (percentiles, probabilities and fan charts), never one
number. Every assumption is printed next to the result. This is the last phase before M3.

Projections are scenarios generated from assumptions. They are not forecasts, and the report
says so.

## Return engines

All engines return simulated **simple** returns as an array of shape
(paths × periods × assets) at one stated frequency. Every engine needs an explicit
`numpy.random.Generator`; there is no default seed and no global random state.

| Engine | Definition |
|---|---|
| Normal | Log returns $\ell_t \sim N(\mu, \Sigma)$ i.i.d., simple returns $e^{\ell_t}-1$. Working in logs keeps every simple return above −100 %. |
| Student-t | $\ell_t = \mu + \sqrt{(\nu-2)/\nu}\; L z_t \sqrt{\nu/\chi^2_\nu}$, with $LL^\top = \Sigma$. The covariance is $\Sigma$, the tails are fatter, and $\nu > 2$ is required. |
| Block bootstrap | Whole rows of historical simple returns, resampled in stationary-bootstrap blocks (Politis–Romano), with an explicit mean block length. This keeps the cross-section and short-range dependence. History is reused, so the paths cannot contain moves that history never had. |

- **Estimating inputs.** `log_moments(returns, kind, missing)` estimates $\mu$ and $\Sigma$ of
  log returns from history. The number of observations is reported, and the estimation
  uncertainty of $\mu$ is shown in the sensitivity section.
- **Regime switching** (optional in the roadmap) is listed in `docs/IDEAS.md`.

## The plan (cash-flow projection)

A `Plan` states everything explicitly. Wealth is tracked per path and per asset, together with
its average cost basis.

- **Start:** initial wealth in the base currency, target weights, and the frequency with
  `periods_per_year`.
- **Flows:** a list of items, each with a period range and an amount per period:
  - regular contributions (PAC);
  - lump sums (a single period);
  - withdrawals, given as the **net** amount to spend; taxes come out of wealth on top of it.

  Each flow is either fixed in nominal terms or indexed to inflation (explicit flag).
- **Timing:** `flow_timing` is `"start"` or `"end"` of each period, with no default.
- **Order within a period:**
  1. flows at `"start"`;
  2. the return;
  3. costs;
  4. flows at `"end"`;
  5. rebalancing.
- **Contributions** are invested at the target weights. **Withdrawals** are sold in
  proportion to current holdings.
- **Rebalancing:** `"none"` (buy and hold) or every `k` periods back to the target weights.
- **Costs:** an annual cost rate charged pro rata each period on wealth (platform or advice
  fees). Fund TERs belong in the return assumptions, and the note on the report says so.
- **Inflation:** a deterministic annual rate. It is used to index flows and to express wealth in
  real terms (today's money). Stochastic inflation is listed in `docs/IDEAS.md`.
- **Taxes:** an explicit `TaxModel`:
  - `capital_gains_rate`, applied to gains realised by withdrawals and rebalancing sales. The
    average cost basis is tracked per asset, as in the Italian *regime amministrato*.
  - `wealth_tax_rate`: an annual tax on value, charged pro rata. An example is the Italian stamp
    duty of 0.20 %.
  - **Losses are not carried forward** in this version. That overstates taxes slightly, and the
    report says so. The four-year carry-forward goes to `docs/IDEAS.md`.
  - The example values for Italy (26 %; 12.5 % on government bonds, blended by the user; 0.20 %
    stamp duty) are documented examples, not tax advice.
- **Depletion:** a path is depleted in the first period whose withdrawal (plus tax) exceeds its
  wealth. The remaining wealth is paid out, and wealth stays at 0 afterwards. The output also
  reports the shortfall.

## Outputs

- **Fan chart:** percentiles 5, 25, 50, 75 and 95 of wealth per period, nominal and real.
- **Terminal distribution:**
  - mean and percentiles, nominal and real;
  - the value after liquidation tax (selling everything at the end) next to the pre-tax value.
- **Goal:**
  - $P(\text{real terminal wealth} \ge \text{goal})$;
  - $P(\text{depleted})$, with the distribution of the depletion period.
- **Sequence-of-returns risk:** paths are grouped by the portfolio return of their first
  `early_periods`. The output shows the median terminal wealth of the worst and the best
  deciles of early returns, which shows how much the order of returns matters for a plan with
  flows.
- **Monte Carlo error:** every probability carries its standard error
  $\sqrt{p(1-p)/N}$, and every mean its standard error $s/\sqrt{N}$. Percentiles are reported
  with $N$.
- **Sensitivity:** the same plan run under one-at-a-time changes, with common random numbers
  so that differences come from the assumptions, not from noise:
  - mean return ±1 percentage point a year;
  - volatility ×0.8 and ×1.2;
  - inflation ±1 point;
  - costs ±0.5 point.

  Each change shows the goal probability and the median terminal wealth.

## Report section "Projection"

An optional input of `build_portfolio_report`; `None` writes "not computed".

- A fan-chart figure (real wealth).
- Tables:
  - the assumptions;
  - the terminal distribution;
  - the goal and depletion probabilities with their standard errors;
  - sequence risk;
  - sensitivity.
- A note says that projections are scenarios from assumptions, not forecasts or advice.

## Layering

New package `simulation`, layer 5. `ALLOWED`:

- `simulation`: `core`, `stats`, `metrics`;
- `reporting` also gains `simulation`.

The bootstrap engine draws its own block indices for a horizon of any length, with the same
scheme as `stats.stationary_bootstrap_indices`, whose resamples have the length of the sample.

## Edge cases

- **Plan:** zero paths or periods (raise); weights that do not sum to 1 (raise, never
  rescaled); a flow outside the horizon (raise); a withdrawal from zero wealth (depleted at
  once).
- **Engines:** a covariance that is not positive semi-definite (raise); $\nu \le 2$ (raise); a
  history with fewer rows than 2 (raise); NaN in the history, with the explicit missing-value
  policy.
- **Memory:** paths × periods × assets is held in memory. A limit (default stated: 50 million
  cells, an explicit parameter) raises before a run that would not fit.

## Risks

- **Assumptions dominate.** The mean return is the least certain input, so the sensitivity
  table and the estimation window are always shown.
- **i.i.d. engines** ignore volatility clustering and mean reversion. The bootstrap keeps short
  dependence only. Both limits are stated.
- **Tax model simplifications** (no loss carry-forward, one blended rate) are stated. The
  output is not tax advice.
- **Monte Carlo noise** is reported, not hidden.
