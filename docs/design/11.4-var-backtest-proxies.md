# Design note 11.4 — VaR backtest and factor proxies in the portfolio report

Status: **approved 2026-11-17.** Fourth phase of Level 11 (`docs/design/levels-10-13-plan.md`).

## The problem

The report's risk section (8.3) gives VaR and ES by method, and stress tests. Two pieces that
the library has are not used there:

- **No check of the VaR model.** The section says what the VaR is, but not whether the model
  would have been right. The `risk_report` kind has a VaR backtest (Kupiec 1995,
  Christoffersen 1998: `qrlab.risk.var_backtest`), but it reads its own returns file, not the
  report's portfolio.
- **Uncovered positions in the historical stress tests.** A historical stress test of a
  position whose price history starts after the crisis is "not covered" (8.3). Its weight is
  left out, never assumed to lose 0.
  - **The engine can already do better.** It proxies a position by factor returns and betas
    (method `proxy`, 3.2), and `scenario_analysis` uses that.
  - **The report gives it no factors.** So an ETF launched in 2015 is not covered by 2008.
  - **The same holds for hypothetical factor shocks:** without betas no position maps to them.

## The change

Both pieces are optional, under `[parameters.risk]`. Without them the kind runs as today, byte
for byte.

### 1. The VaR backtest: `[parameters.risk.backtest]`

```toml
[parameters.risk.backtest]
window = 36          # returns per forecast
model = "normal"     # "historical", "normal" or "cornish_fisher", as in risk_report
```

- **The series:** today's weights applied to the full common history of the held positions'
  returns, at the risk frequency (the "what if this portfolio had been held" of 8.3). The
  returns are those of 11.1, with income where it is known.
- **The forecasts:** `var_forecasts` gives, for each date, the VaR of the `window` returns
  strictly before it, at the section's `confidence`. `var_backtest` then gives:
  - the number of forecasts and of exceedances;
  - the expected exceedances;
  - the Kupiec, independence and conditional-coverage statistics with their p-values.
- **How much data it needs:**
  - The history must give at least 2 forecasts, or the run raises and says how many returns
    it has. That is the library's own minimum.
  - **Too few to judge:** when the expected number of exceedances, $n(1 - c)$, is below 5,
    the section says that the tests have little power. Five is the usual rule of thumb for the
    $\chi^2$ approximation. With monthly returns at 95 % that is fewer than 100 forecasts;
    the numbers are still shown.
- **What it is, written next to it.** It checks the VaR **model** on today's weights held
  constant over the past. It is not a record of past risk, and weights chosen today carry
  hindsight. A test can only reject; not rejecting is not proof that the model is right.
- **Outputs:**
  - `var_backtest.csv` (`return`, `var`, `exceedance`);
  - the scalars `backtest_exceedances`, `backtest_expected`, `kupiec_pvalue`,
    `independence_pvalue` and `conditional_coverage_pvalue`, named as in `risk_report`;
  - a table in the risk section, in English and Italian.
  - **The tag** is `out_of_sample`, as in `risk_report`: each forecast uses only earlier
    returns. The hindsight in the weights is stated.

### 2. Factor proxies: `factors` input and `[parameters.risk.proxies]`

- **`factors` and `factors_mapping`:** a price file of factor levels (phase 1.2).
  - **The series:** total-return series, `price_type = "total_return"`, as the benchmark of
    11.2. One column per factor, named by its mapping key, for example a world equity index
    and a euro government bond index.
  - **The returns:** the factor returns are the file's simple returns, on its own dates. The
    engine compounds them over each scenario's window, as it does a position's own returns.
- **`[parameters.risk.proxies]`:** for each proxied ISIN, its betas to the factors:

  ```toml
  [parameters.risk.proxies."IE0000000012"]
  "World equity" = 1.0
  ```

  - **Betas are the owner's.** Estimating them from the overlap of the position's returns
    and the factors is a modelling choice of its own (in IDEAS). For an index ETF, beta 1 to
    its own index is the plain choice.
  - **The engine's rule stays:** a position's own series is used whenever it covers the
    window. The proxy is used only when it does not, and for hypothetical factor shocks.
- **Currency:**
  - **The engine's convention:** it maps $r_{i,t} = \beta_i^\top f_t$, then applies the
    currency effect of the position's exposure currency.
  - **So the currencies must match:** the factors file's currency must equal the exposure
    currency of every proxied position (its hedge currency, or the fund's currency). Otherwise
    the run raises, rather than count a currency effect twice or not at all.
- **The report:**
  - the stress-test detail already shows each position's method; `proxy` is shown as "proxy
    (factor betas)";
  - a new small table lists each proxied position, its factors and betas;
  - the sentence under the stress tests says that proxies are linear maps with no residual,
    so they understate a position's own risk.

### 3. The API

- **No new public function or field.** The work is in the private
  `qrlab.experiments.kinds._portfolio_risk`, which already builds the engine's
  `MarketData`; it now fills `factors` and `betas`.
- **The report table of the backtest** needs the result in the report. `RiskSummary`
  (public) gains an optional `backtest: VarBacktest | None = None` and
  `proxies: Mapping[Isin, Mapping[str, float]] | None = None`: additions with defaults, as
  after the freeze.

## Edge cases (each raises, naming the key or the ISIN)

- **Backtest settings:** a `window` below 2; an unknown `model`.
- **Backtest data:** a history giving fewer than 2 forecasts.
- **The factors file:** a column that is not `total_return`; its currency different from a
  proxied position's exposure currency.
- **The proxies:**
  - a proxied ISIN that is not held;
  - a beta for a factor that is not in the file, or a beta that is not a finite number;
  - `proxies` without a `factors` input, or a `factors` input without `proxies`.
- **A factor that does not cover a scenario's window:** that position stays "not covered",
  as the engine already does, with its note. This does not raise.

## Tests, hand-calculated first

1. **The backtest by hand.** Portfolio returns (1, −1, 2, 0, −5, 1, 3, −4) %, window 4, normal
   model at 95 % ($z = 1.644854$).
   - **The first forecast:** mean 0.5 %, standard deviation $\sqrt{5/3}\,\% = 1.290994\,\%$,
     so a VaR of $-0.5 + 1.644854 \times 1.290994 = 1.6235\,\%$. The return of −5 % is an
     exceedance.
   - **The next three forecasts:** 5.8423 %, 5.6141 % and 5.8481 %. None is exceeded.
   - **The tests:** $n = 4$, $x = 1$, expected 0.2, and Kupiec
     $LR = -2[3\ln 0.95 + \ln 0.05 - 3\ln 0.75 - \ln 0.25] = 1.8005$, with p-value 0.1796.
     The "little power" sentence is shown.
2. **The same series through `risk_report`:** the same forecasts and statistics, a
   cross-check between the two kinds.
3. **A proxy by hand.**
   - **The setup:** a fund whose prices start in 2026, a historical scenario in 2025, and a
     factor that returns −10 % then +5 % in 2025 with beta 0.8.
   - **The proxied return:** $(1 - 0.08)(1 + 0.04) - 1 = -4.32\,\%$, with method "proxy".
   - **The other fund,** with its own 2025 prices, keeps "direct".
4. **A hypothetical factor shock** of −20 % with beta 0.8 gives −16 % for the proxied fund.
5. **Each error above,** and the tables in both languages.
6. **Without the new keys** every output is as today: a regression test, and byte identity
   against `main` on the example configs.

## Risks

- **A short history makes the backtest weak.** The section prints $n$, the expected
  exceedances and the "little power" sentence; it never says "the model is validated".
- **Betas are assumptions.** The table shows them next to the stress results, and the
  scenario detail labels each proxied row.

## Out of scope (in `IDEAS.md`)

- **Betas estimated** from the overlap of a position's returns with the factors, with their
  standard errors.
- **Using proxies to extend the backtest history** to periods before a position existed.
- **ES backtests** (for example Acerbi–Székely).

## Results and deviations (2026-11-17)

- **Built as designed:**
  - `[parameters.risk.backtest]` and `[parameters.risk.proxies]` in
    `qrlab.experiments.kinds._portfolio_risk`;
  - the `factors` input in the kind;
  - the backtest and proxies tables in the risk section, in English and Italian;
  - `RiskSummary`'s optional `backtest`, `backtest_window`, `backtest_model` and `proxies`.
- **The common history** is the last run of periods in which every holding has a return, up
  to the period of the report's date. Rows are never joined across a gap.
- **A factor that a proxied position does not list has beta 0,** since the engine needs one
  betas table for all proxied positions.
- **The method label stays "Factor proxy"** (Italian "Proxy fattoriale"), the label the
  stress-test detail already had since 3.2. The design's "proxy (factor betas)" says the same.
- **Cornish-Fisher in the backtest:** a window whose expansion is not a quantile stops the run
  with the library's reason, as in `risk_report`. On short or periodic series this is common.
  The model is still tested directly, and skipping such forecasts is in IDEAS.
- **Tests:**
  - **The backtest:** the hand case (forecasts 1.6235, 5.8423, 5.6141 and 5.8481 %; Kupiec p
    0.1796; the low-power sentence); the report against the library for the normal and
    historical models on a longer series, with and without the low-power sentence; the
    Cornish-Fisher model against the library; too few forecasts; the Italian table.
  - **The proxy:** by hand, historical (−4.32 % for the proxied fund, −2.16 % for the
    portfolio) and hypothetical (−16 %).
  - **Errors:** 9 proxy errors and 3 backtest-setting errors.
- **The gate:** 2635 tests on Python 3.13 and 3.11, with 100 % coverage; the docs and their
  doctests build.
- **Byte identity against `main`:** every output of the 8 example configs is equal, since none
  sets a backtest or proxies.
