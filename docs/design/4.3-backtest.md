# Design note — Phase 4.3 `qrlab.backtest`: the backtest engine

Status: **draft, awaiting approval.**

## Goal

Turn a strategy into a portfolio path with honest timing, costs and taxes. The engine runs:

> signals → target weights → trades (policy, lag) → realised weights (drift) → returns

and reports **gross and net results side by side**.

A backtest is a description of one past path, not evidence that a strategy works. The engine
reports what happened, and the studies (Level 5) add the inference.

## Timing convention (the core rule)

- **Returns.** Return $r_t$ runs from the close of period $t-1$ to the close of period $t$.
- **Decisions.** A strategy decides at the close of $t$, seeing **only** the returns up to and
  including $t$.
- **Execution lag.** `execution_lag` $L$ is required, with no default, and must satisfy
  $L \ge 1$. The trade executes at the close of $t + L$, so the new weights earn
  $r_{t+L+1}$ onward.
  - $L = 0$, trading at the same close the signal was computed on, is refused.
  - This matches the existing module docstring: a signal observed at the close of $t$ can only
    determine weights traded at $t+1$ or later.
- **Strategy interface (the default API).** The strategy is a function
  `strategy(history) -> target weights | None` (`None` keeps the previous target).
  - `history` is a read-only copy of the returns **truncated at $t$**, so the strategy cannot
    index the future through it.
  - It is called on a decision schedule `decide_every` (explicit).
- **Precomputed targets.** `run_backtest_targets(returns, targets, ...)` accepts a frame of
  target weights indexed by **decision date**, for example from walk-forward optimisation
  (4.2 with 4.1). The same lag applies.
  - The caller is responsible for how the targets were computed, and the result records that
    the targets were precomputed.
- **What cannot be enforced.** A closure that captured the full return frame before the run can
  still leak. The docs say so, and the leak test below shows what such a leak does to the
  result.

## Mechanics (per period)

1. Holdings grow with their returns, $h_i \leftarrow h_i(1 + r_{i,t})$, so weights **drift**
   between trades.
2. A pending target whose execution date is $t$ is passed to the **rebalancing policy** from
   4.2:
   - `CalendarPolicy`, `ThresholdPolicy`, or `TradeOnSignal` (trade whenever a new target is
     due, added here);
   - the policy decides whether and to what to trade.
3. **Costs:** `CostModel` from 4.2, a proportional rate plus a fixed fee per asset traded. They
   are charged at the trade and taken pro rata from the holdings after the trade.
4. **Taxes** (optional `TaxModel` from 3.3): capital-gains tax on gains realised by sales, with
   an average cost basis per asset. Gains and losses are not netted across assets and are not
   carried forward, as in 3.3. The tax is paid pro rata from the holdings.
5. **Gross and net ledgers.** Two ledgers run in parallel on the same decisions:
   - **gross**: no costs, no taxes;
   - **net**: costs and taxes.

   Both are reported for every statistic.

**Inputs:**

- simple asset returns on a validated index with no NaN. Assets that start or stop trading are
  listed in `docs/IDEAS.md` as survivorship handling;
- a cash asset is an ordinary column with its own returns, such as €STR, if wanted;
- `initial_weights` and `initial_value` (money with currency), both explicit;
- `periods_per_year`, used only for annualised statistics.

## Output: `BacktestResult`

- Value paths and period returns, gross and net.
- Realised weights per period, the trades per execution date, turnover, costs paid, taxes paid
  and the number of trades.
- Summary statistics, gross and net: total and annualised return (only for periods of at least
  a year), annualised volatility, maximum drawdown and average annual turnover. The 1.4 metrics
  are reused.
- The run settings: lag, decision schedule, policy, costs, taxes, and whether the targets were
  computed by a strategy or precomputed.

## Layering

`backtest` already sits at layer 4 with `core`, `data`, `metrics` and `validation`. It gains
`optimization` (policies and costs) and `simulation` (`TaxModel`). `reporting` gains
`backtest`, which is already allowed.

## Hand-calculated tests (three-asset toy)

Returns of A, B and C over four periods:

| Period | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +10 % | 0 % | −5 % |
| 2 | −5 % | +5 % | 0 % |
| 3 | 0 % | 0 % | +10 % |
| 4 | +5 % | −5 % | 0 % |

Starting value 300, initial weights 1/3 each.

| Case | Expected |
|---|---|
| Buy and hold (no trades) | values 305.000, 304.500, 314.000, 313.975; total +4.6583 % |
| Rebalance to 1/3 at the close of periods 1–3, no costs | values 305.000, 305.000, 315.1667, 315.1667; total +5.0556 % |
| Same with a 0.10 % proportional cost | net values 304.9833, 304.9732, 315.1254, 315.1254; costs 0.040387; gross equal to the row above |
| A signal at the close of period 1 goes all into C, $L$ = 1 | trades at the close of 2 and earns $r_3$ and $r_4$ on C: total +11.65 % |
| The same signal with no lag (the leak) | +11.83 %, a different result; the API refuses $L = 0$ |
| A strategy that reads `history` beyond $t$ | raises (index out of range); no future data is visible |
| A sale realising a gain of 10 at 26 % tax | tax 2.60 in the net ledger only |
| Threshold policy, band 5 % | trades only on the dates where a weight leaves the band |

## Edge cases

- **Inputs:** an empty or single-period history; NaN returns (raise); targets with unknown
  assets, negative weights when long-only is required, or weights that do not sum to 1 (raise);
  initial weights that do not sum to 1 (raise).
- **Timing:** a decision whose execution date falls after the last period is recorded as not
  executed; `decide_every` must be at least 1.
- **Strategies:** a strategy that returns NaN or the wrong labels raises, naming the decision
  date.

## Risks

- **Leakage through features.** Covered by the truncated history, $L \ge 1$ and the leak test.
  The limit for closures is stated plainly.
- **Costs that are too low** make results look better than they are. Costs are explicit, and
  gross and net are always shown together.
- **Overfitting by repeated backtests.** The engine counts nothing. The studies (Level 5)
  record every configuration tried and apply multiple-testing corrections.
