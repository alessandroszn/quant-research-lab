# Design note — Phase 5.3, study 2: 1/N against optimised portfolios

Status: **approved 2026-11-12.** Deviations, if any, are listed at the end.

## Question

DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal (2009, *RFS*) find that none of 14 sample-based optimised
portfolios beats the naive 1/N portfolio out of sample, in Sharpe ratio, certainty equivalent or
turnover, once estimation error is accounted for. They argue that the estimation window needed
for mean-variance to win is far longer than the data available.

The extension asks whether methods built to resist estimation error close that gap:

- Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage;
- risk parity;
- hierarchical risk parity (HRP);
- Black–Litterman (BL), which shrinks sample means toward equilibrium.

**Hypotheses, stated before running:**

- **H1 (replication).** No optimised strategy has a significantly higher out-of-sample Sharpe
  ratio than 1/N after costs. The correction is Holm across the strategies, within each dataset,
  window and cost.
- **H2 (robust estimation).** Robust estimation helps a strategy relative to its sample-estimate
  version. Two pairs are tested:
  - minimum variance with Ledoit–Wolf against minimum variance with the sample covariance;
  - maximum Sharpe with the BL posterior against maximum Sharpe with sample moments.

  In each pair the robust version has the higher net Sharpe ratio.

An honest null result is a valid result and is reported as such.

## Method

### Timing and execution

- **Decisions** are made at the last trading day of each month. They use the monthly excess
  returns of the previous $M$ months only, on a rolling window as in the paper, with
  $M \in \{60, 120\}$.
- **Execution** goes through the existing backtest engine (`run_backtest_targets`) on **daily**
  total returns:
  - execution lag of one trading day;
  - every strategy rebalances to its target each month (`TradeOnSignal`);
  - proportional costs of 0, 10 and 50 bp of the amount traded (the paper uses 50 bp);
  - gross and net ledgers side by side.
- **Deviation from the paper:** DGU apply the weights to the same month's return, with no
  lag. The project requires a lag, and one trading day is the smallest one.
- **Performance:** monthly net returns are compounded from the daily net returns, then the
  monthly RF is subtracted. Every comparison uses the same out-of-sample months, from the
  first decision with $M = 120$, so the two windows are comparable.

### Strategies

All strategies are fully invested and long-only. The names are fixed now.

| Name | Weights | Inputs |
|---|---|---|
| `ew` | 1/N | — |
| `vw` | value weights (the BL prior with no views) | lagged market weights |
| `maxsharpe_sample` | maximum Sharpe ratio, long-only (DGU "mv-c") | sample mean and covariance |
| `minvar_sample` | minimum variance, long-only (DGU "min-c") | sample covariance |
| `minvar_lw` | minimum variance, long-only | Ledoit–Wolf covariance |
| `maxsharpe_bl` | maximum Sharpe ratio, long-only | BL posterior mean and covariance |
| `risk_parity` | equal risk contributions | Ledoit–Wolf covariance |
| `hrp` | hierarchical risk parity (López de Prado 2016) | sample covariance |

**Black–Litterman settings** (`maxsharpe_bl`):

- prior $\pi=\delta\Sigma w_{mkt}$ with $\delta=2.5$;
- base covariance $\Sigma$ = Ledoit–Wolf;
- one view per asset ($P=I$), with the window's sample means as view returns ($q$);
- $\Omega=\operatorname{diag}(\tau P\Sigma P^\top)$ and $\tau=1/M$.

So BL shrinks the sample means toward the equilibrium returns, and the shrinkage is weaker for
longer windows.

**Maximum Sharpe, long-only.** This is a new library function,
`optimization.maximum_sharpe`. It minimises $y^\top\Sigma y$ subject to $\mu^\top y=1$ and
$y\ge 0$, then sets $w=y/\mathbf 1^\top y$ (Cornuejols and Tütüncü). It needs at least one
positive expected excess return. Otherwise the maximum Sharpe ratio is negative and the
transformation fails, so the strategy falls back to minimum variance for that month. The
report counts these months.

### Evaluation

For each dataset, window, cost and strategy, out of sample:

- annualised mean excess return, volatility and Sharpe ratio;
- certainty-equivalent return, $\text{CEQ}=12(\hat\mu-\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\hat\sigma^2)$ with
  $\gamma=1$, as in the paper;
- average monthly turnover from the engine's executions;
- maximum drawdown.

**Tests:**

- **Against 1/N:** Sharpe-ratio difference by the Ledoit–Wolf HAC test (6 lags; primary) and
  by Jobson–Korkie–Memmel (to compare with the paper). Also a stationary-bootstrap 95 %
  interval of the Sharpe difference and of the CEQ difference.
- **H1:** Holm and Benjamini–Hochberg across the 7 strategies, within each dataset, window and
  cost.
- **H2:** the two pairs, HAC test, Holm across the two pairs within each dataset, window and
  cost.
- **Deflated Sharpe ratio** of the best variant: $N$ = variants in the run × configurations in
  the trial ledger, as in study 1.

**Configurations:** two only, both fixed in this note:

- main: 1963-07 to the end of the vintage;
- paper period: 1963-07 to 2004-12.

## Implementation

- **`data.ken_french`** (data layer; the existing behaviour is unchanged):
  - the four new datasets;
  - section selection by title: the files contain several monthly sections, such as value- and
    equal-weighted returns, number of firms and average firm size;
  - a function for non-return sections (counts, size in USD millions), with the right unit.
  - The parser stays strict: an unknown or ambiguous title raises.

  raise, because the transformation does not preserve them.
- **`studies.one_over_n`** (pure functions):
  - monthly excess returns;
  - lagged market weights;
  - rolling-window targets per strategy;
  - running the engine;
  - performance and the tables with corrections.
- **Experiment kind `study_one_over_n`:**
  - inputs: the four portfolio files and the factor file;
  - parameters: datasets, windows, strategies, costs, the BL settings, HAC lags, bootstrap,
    significance and the sample window;
  - outputs: tagged tables, the HTML report and the record.
- **Written report** `docs/studies/02-one-over-n.md`, written after the run with the actual

## Risks

- **Specification search:** strategies, windows, costs and BL settings are fixed here. Every
  run is counted in the ledger and the DSR uses that count.
- **Unequal data quality:** the 25 size and book-to-market portfolios include tiny-firm
  portfolios with high costs in practice. A flat cost understates trading costs for them.
  This is stated as a limitation.
- **File format:** the parser extension is tested on synthetic files built from the documented
  layout. If the real files differ, the strict parser raises rather than misreading them, and
  the fix goes into PR (b) with a regression test.
- **Optimiser failures:** the solvers can fail to converge. A failure stops the run with a
  clear error; months are never silently skipped.
- **Run time:** 2 datasets × 2 windows × 3 costs × 8 strategies = 96 backtests. Targets are
  computed once per dataset, window and strategy, then reused for each cost. The engine takes
  under a second per daily backtest.
